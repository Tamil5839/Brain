# A THOUGHT — notes

A film of a whole-brain simulation of the fruit fly *Drosophila melanogaster*, built on the complete published wiring diagram (FlyWire, release 783). Every flash of light in the film is a spike produced by the simulation; every number on screen comes from the data or from the simulation output. This file is generated by `python notes.py` from the pipeline's result files.

On-screen wording used throughout: *"A simulation built on the real wiring of a fruit fly's brain"* (never "a real fly's thoughts").

Sensory neurons have their somata outside the brain, so they are drawn at their anchor point where they enter the brain.
- Coordinates are 4 × 4 × 40 nm voxels, converted to nm and then to a render space centred on the brain mesh and scaled so the largest half-extent is 1 (scale 2.3830e-06 per nm; centre 491,510, 252,470, 140,830 nm). ## 4. Connectivity and transmitter signs

- Connections (neuron pairs with ≥ 1 synapse): **15,091,983**; synapses: **54,492,922**; 9,059,302 excitatory, 6,032,681 inhibitory. No synapse threshold is applied (the authors' v783 table contains every pair, minimum 1 synapse). For reference: 2,700,513 connections have ≥ 5 synapses (34,153,566 synapses).
- Weight of a connection = sign(presynaptic neuron) × synapse count × w_syn. The sign is the authors' `Excitatory` column (constant per presynaptic neuron): acetylcholine → excitatory, GABA and glutamate → inhibitory, other transmitters (dopamine, serotonin, octopamine) → excitatory (as in the paper and in flypoke).
- The authors' signs disagree with the per-neuron `top_nt` of annotation v3.1.0 for 6,024 neurons (e.g. 1,719 ACh neurons are inhibitory and 2,943 glutamate / 1,349 GABA neurons excitatory in the authors' table), presumably because the authors aggregated the synapse-level transmitter predictions differently. The simulation follows the authors' file ("exactly as implemented"); the colour of a spike shows what the neuron does in the simulation (section 7). flypoke's convention (annotation `top_nt`) is evaluated as a cross-check.

## 5. Model

`sim/model.py` re-implements, step by step, the Brian2 model in the authors' `model.py` (commit `91bdd1e`), with a scipy.sparse CSR matrix for synaptic input and vectorised NumPy updates. Every parameter is taken from `default_params` in that file (a unit test parses the file and compares):

| parameter | value | authors' comment |
|---|---|---|
| `t_run` | 1000 ms | duration of trial |
| `n_run` | 30 | number of runs |
| `v_0` | -52 mV | resting potential |
| `v_rst` | -52 mV | reset potential after spike |
| `v_th` | -45 mV | threshold for spiking |
| `t_mbr` | 20 ms | membrane time scale |
| `tau` | 5 ms | time constant (synaptic) |
| `t_rfc` | 2.2 ms | refractory period |
| `t_dly` | 1.8 ms | delay for changes in post-synaptic neuron |
| `w_syn` | 0.275 mV | weight per synapse (free parameter) |
| `r_poi` | 150 Hz | rate of the Poisson inputs |
| `f_poi` | 250 | scaling factor for Poisson synapse |
| `dt` | 0.1 ms | Brian2 default clock (model.py does not set it) |

Equations (verbatim): `dv/dt = (v_0 - v + g) / t_mbr : volt (unless refractory)`, `dg/dt = -g / tau : volt (unless refractory)`; threshold `v > v_th`; reset `v = v_rst; g = 0`; `Synapses(on_pre='g += w', delay=t_dly)`; `PoissonInput(target_var='v', N=1, rate=r_poi, weight=w_syn*f_poi)`; stimulated neurons have refractory period 0; integration `method='linear'` (exact).

Per 0.1 ms step, in Brian2's schedule order: (1) exact update of (v, g) for non-refractory neurons, refractory neurons frozen; (2) threshold; (3) delayed synaptic events (round(1.8 / 0.1) = 18 steps) are added to g — **except for postsynaptic neurons that are refractory**: Brian2 masks synaptic updates of variables flagged `(unless refractory)`, so input arriving during the refractory period is discarded (found by comparing with Brian2 and confirmed in Brian2's generated code: `add.at(g, post[not_refractory], w[not_refractory])`); Poisson input adds 68.75 mV to v with probability rate × dt; (4) reset. A Poisson event therefore forces a spike one step later.

Recording: every spike of every neuron (step index, neuron index), per trial, stored compressed in `results/spikes/<condition>.npz`.

## 6. Stimuli, experiments and validation

### Stimulus identification

- Annotation classes (v3.1.0), identical to flypoke's `feeding` selectors: `cell_sub_class == 'sugar/water'` 129 neurons (67 left, 62 right), `cell_sub_class == 'bitter'` 65 (32 left, 33 right), ingestion motor neurons 28. In the paper-version annotation v2.1.0 these classes have 131 and 42 neurons.
- **MN9**: there is no `cell_type == 'MN9'` in any annotation version. Shiu et al. use root id 720575940660219265 for MN9 (figures.ipynb); in release 783 that id is unchanged and annotated `cell_type` **CB0701** (ingestion motor neuron). CB0701 has exactly 2 neurons, one per side (720575940618238523, 720575940660219265; sides left, right); both are treated as MN9, as in flypoke.
- **Film stimuli: the GRN lists of Shiu et al.** (figures.ipynb, snapshot 630): 21 sugar-sensing and 21 bitter-sensing labellar GRNs; 20 and 20 of these root ids exist unchanged in release 783 and are used (all annotated `sugar/water` and `bitter` respectively). The annotation cannot separate sugar from water GRNs (both are cell type LB3); the paper identified sugar GRNs by connectivity. The paper calls them "right hemisphere"; annotation v3.1.0 lists all of them as side = left (and Shiu et al.'s "left" MN9 as right), i.e. the two sources name the sides in mirror image. The film avoids calling the input left or right; the MN9 readout uses the annotation's sides.
- **Why not the whole annotation classes for the film:** in the exact model (no synapse threshold) stimulating all 129 sugar/water GRNs also drives the olfactory system and mushroom bodies into sustained activity (section 6c, last table): thousands of neurons fire, a regime not reported in the paper and not a depiction of tasting sugar. The paper's own stimulus sets give the published focal response. The annotation classes are run as documented cross-checks.

## 7. The film: what is data and what is a visual aid

**Data (from the simulation or the connectome):**

- Every neuron is one point at its true position (section 3). Resting neurons are dim grey-blue points of constant apparent size.
- Every flash is a spike of the representative trial of the condition shown. The glow of neuron i at brain time t is Σ exp(−(t − s)/8 ms) over its spikes s ≤ t (≈ 95 % decayed after 25 ms of brain time), evaluated at every sub-frame from the stored spike times. No spike is added, removed or moved.
- Spike colour: amber = excitatory, cyan = inhibitory in the model (authors' sign), soft white = neurons predicted to use dopamine, serotonin or octopamine (excitatory in the model), green = stimulated sugar GRNs, violet = stimulated bitter GRNs, white with a ring marker = MN9.
- Brain clock: brain time of the trial in ms; slow-motion factor shown on screen (25× in the master, 10× in the vertical cut); film time = brain time × factor.
- MN9 readout: spikes of each MN9 neuron in the last 100 ms of brain time × 10 (Hz); after the trial the total spike count of the trial. The end-of-C comparison shows the MN9 spike counts of the two representative trials.
- Long exposure: every neuron that spiked in trial A, B or C is drawn in that trial's colour with brightness ∝ √(its spike count), accumulating in brain-time order (the three 1 s trials replayed together over 10 s of film). During it the resting neurons fade to 12 % so that the points of light are the spikes; the glass shell keeps the brain's outline.

**Visual aids (not part of the model):**

- **Pathway highlight.** 24 neurons chosen from the representative sugar trial: MN9 (2), the 8 neurons with the largest (spikes × excitatory synapses onto MN9), the 8 with the largest (spikes × synapses onto those), and the 6 stimulated sugar GRNs with the largest input to them. Their skeletons are drawn as thin lines, dim when silent and lit with the same spike glow as their points when they fire. Arcs are drawn for every excitatory connection of ≥ 5 synapses from one level to the next (49 arcs). **When a pathway neuron spikes, a small pulse travels along each of its arcs in 30 ms of film time. This travel is a visual aid: the model has no propagation along wires, only a fixed 1.8 ms synaptic delay.**
- **Sugar glow before experiment A.** While the camera glides to the taste region, the 20 sugar GRNs are marked by a steady soft green glow (a label, not activity); it fades before the trial starts.
- **Fly inset.** A line drawing labelled "illustration". Its proboscis extension follows the simulated MN9 rate (mean of both MN9, last 50 ms of brain time; 60 Hz = fully extended). The model has no muscles or behaviour; MN9 is the motor neuron that extends the proboscis in real flies.
- Brain shell (Fresnel rim), bloom, ACES tone mapping, grain, camera motion and 4-sub-frame motion blur are presentation only.

## 8. Rendering

moderngl (OpenGL 3.3 core) through a headless EGL context. This build machine has no GPU, so Mesa's llvmpipe rasterised on the CPU. Points and lines are drawn additively into a float32 HDR buffer; 4 sub-frames per output frame (180° shutter) are accumulated for motion blur; the brain shell once per frame. Post: 6-level bloom (13-tap downsample, tent upsample), ACES filmic tone curve (Narkowicz fit), gamma, background #030407, fine grain. Overlays (Inter) are composited in sRGB. Encoding: ffmpeg libx264, yuv420p, BT.709; the 1080p version is a Lanczos downscale of the master. `render/cpu.py` is a slow NumPy splatting fallback for previews without GL.

QA: one defect appeared during development — a single non-finite pixel from the shell shader (`pow` of a slightly negative base when |n·v| rounds above 1) that the bloom chain spread into a dark block. The shader now clamps its input and the bloom and final passes discard non-finite values; `tests/qa_video.py --nan master 40` re-renders 40 random frames at full resolution and confirms the HDR buffer is finite in every one.

## 11. Limitations

- The model is a point-neuron LIF model without plasticity, neuromodulation, gap junctions or behaviour; it shows what the wiring alone does under these assumptions, as in Shiu et al.
- The official Zenodo connection table and skeleton archive were unreachable from the build environment; see section 2 for the substitutes and the evidence that they match.
- The FlyWire data licence wording could not be checked against the primary source from this environment.
- 1 of the paper's 21 sugar GRNs and 1 of its 21 bitter GRNs no longer exist under the same root id in release 783 and are not stimulated.
